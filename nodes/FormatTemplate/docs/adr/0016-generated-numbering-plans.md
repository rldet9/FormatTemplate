# ADR-0016 — Numbering plans of every country, generated from libphonenumber

- **Status:** Accepted — amends decisions 2 to 4 of [ADR-0005](0005-zero-runtime-dependencies.md)
- **Date:** 2026-10-26

## Context

[ADR-0005](0005-zero-runtime-dependencies.md) kept the node free of runtime dependencies,
and so read phone numbers with a short, hand-written table: France, the United States and
Canada. Any other calling code was left untouched with `FT_PHONE_PLAN_UNSUPPORTED`. It named
the criterion to revisit that choice: real numbers from other countries flowing through the
node. They now do, and the node is published for anyone to use.

Three ways to read every country's numbers were weighed:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A runtime dependency (`libphonenumber-js`) | Refused: decision 1 of ADR-0005 and n8n's rule for verified community nodes. |
| A longer hand-written table | Refused: 245 territories, lengths that vary within a country (4 to 15 digits in Germany), national prefixes rewritten rather than dropped. Exactly the risk ADR-0005 decision 4 warned about — a wrong length turns valid numbers into errors. |
| A table **generated** from Google's libphonenumber metadata, at a pinned version | **Chosen.** The approach of [ADR-0009](0009-offline-country-table.md) for country names. |

libphonenumber's metadata (`resources/PhoneNumberMetadata.xml`) is maintained by Google from
the ITU and national regulators, and is what Android and most phone libraries read.

## Decision

1. **Decision 1 of ADR-0005 stands.** No runtime dependency other than `n8n-workflow`; the
   table is data in a TypeScript module. AR-03 and AR-09 are unchanged.

2. *(amends ADR-0005 decision 2)* Phone numbers are read with the numbering plans of **every
   territory** of the metadata — 245 — in `transforms/phone/numberingPlans.ts`, generated by
   `node scripts/gen_phone_plans.js` from a **pinned** libphonenumber release whose file is
   checked by SHA-256. Regenerating is a deliberate change, reviewed through the diff of the
   table. The non-geographic services (+800, +808, +870, +878, +881 to +883, +888, +979) are
   left out: they are not countries.

3. *(amends ADR-0005 decision 3)* `FT_PHONE_PLAN_UNSUPPORTED` remains, for a calling code no
   country holds and for a `defaultCountry` outside the table. The value is still left
   untouched.

4. *(amends ADR-0005 decision 4)* Lengths and patterns come from the metadata, never from a
   hand: a number must have a possible length of its plan **and** match the plan's general
   pattern, or it is refused — `FT_PHONE_LENGTH_MISMATCH`, or the new `FT_PHONE_NOT_IN_PLAN`.
   Reading and rendering follow libphonenumber's own steps: national prefixes dropped or
   rewritten by the plan's rule, the format chosen by leading digits, a shared calling code
   rendered with its main territory's formats.

5. **The node stays stricter than libphonenumber where a value could be approximate**
   ([ADR-0002](0002-never-approximate.md)):
   - a national input without the national prefix its plan writes is refused, unless the
     metadata marks the prefix optional — `320000000` in France is a zero lost upstream;
   - only `+` and `00` mark an international input; no country's own international prefix
     (`011`) and no calling code written without `+` is guessed;
   - when a plan has no format for a number, libphonenumber writes the digits ungrouped. The
     node does so in the international shape, and in the national shape only for a plan
     without a national prefix; otherwise it reports `FT_PHONE_FORMAT_UNAVAILABLE`.

6. **The three plans of the first typeVersion 6 release keep their behaviour**
   ([ADR-0004](0004-no-retroactive-change.md)). The generator replaces, for FR, US and CA
   only, the length, the general pattern and the formats of the metadata with those of that
   release: any nine digits after France's `0`, any ten digits in North America, grouped as
   before. A value the node already formatted is formatted identically — French freephone
   numbers stay `08 00 12 34 56` where libphonenumber writes `0 800 12 34 56`.

7. **Attribution.** The metadata is licensed under the Apache License 2.0. The generated
   module says where it comes from and how it was modified; `PHONE_METADATA` carries the
   source, version, copyright and licence at runtime, since the compiled package keeps no
   comment; the published package ships a `NOTICE` and the licence text.

## Consequences

- The node reads and renders the numbers of 245 territories, with the grouping of each
  country's own convention. On the 1,132 example numbers of the metadata — 3,396 renderings
  and 1,132 national readings — it gives the result of libphonenumber's reference
  implementation, except 17 by decision: the historical plans (French `08` numbers,
  Canada's seven-digit service numbers) and 5 national renderings refused under
  decision 5.
- Behaviour changes within typeVersion 6 for values the node used to refuse: a British
  number that yielded `FT_PHONE_PLAN_UNSUPPORTED` is now formatted. No value the node
  formatted before changes — measured by running both engines on more than 22,000
  combinations of French and North American inputs and settings, and frozen by the tests of
  the historical plans. Two forms the first
  release refused are now read, as libphonenumber reads them: a national prefix written
  after the calling code (`+33 (0)3 20 00 00 00`, `+1 1 212 555 1234`).
- The table weighs about 146 kB; a test bounds it. Its patterns are compiled on first use
  and kept.
- The node normalizes, it does not validate: a number matching its plan's general pattern
  is not necessarily assigned.
- Aligning FR, US and CA on libphonenumber's grouping would change values already
  produced; it needs a new typeVersion.

## Proven by

- `phone-plans.test.ts` — PH-05 (every example number of every plan, read and read back),
  PH-06 (renderings of 26 territories checked against libphonenumber's reference
  implementation), PH-07 (historical plans), PH-08 (the node's own rules and diagnostics),
  PH-09 (origin, size and options of the generated table).
- `phone-format.test.ts` — PH-01 to PH-04.
- `architecture.test.ts` — AR-03 (no package other than `n8n-workflow` in shipped code).
